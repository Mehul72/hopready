### 1.4 What this settles

1. Park-and-ride stays composed by HopReady: a drive estimate plus a public transport query per car park (ADR-0001 holds).
2. The public transport query for a car park can start at its TSN. That removes the padded walking leg and makes the answer the platform's own departures. The walk from the car park to the platform becomes HopReady's own estimate from the facility's coordinate to the stop.
3. The walking limit can be passed to TfNSW as `trITMOTvalue100`, which improves the journeys it proposes, but HopReady must not discard what comes back; TfNSW already returns the most sensible journey even when it breaks the cap.
4. Capacity and live occupancy per facility are available and unused.

### 1.5 The walking limit sent to TfNSW (probed 2026-10-31, 20:30 to 21:00)

- Mechanism (verified): nothing changes until the trip needs a walk longer than N by TfNSW's measure; then TfNSW searches forward, days if needed, for a journey that fits. Every 6 to 42 hour delay was a trip to Anson St ending on school bus 6627 at 15:24 on the next school day. Arrive-by jumps backwards the same way: "arrive by Sunday 10:00" with 15 returned only Friday 15:24 arrivals.
- The vendor documents it as a cap: "the maximum time from the starting point to the first stop and also from the destination stop to the destination point" (Mentz EFA JSON training EN 2.9, page 74, default 10). It is not even a cap HopReady can mirror: 17 accepted an 18 minute walk, 8 kept 16 and 18 minute walks, and 60 added 82 minute walks.
- Without it (verified): no hard cap. The longest walk in the 36 default answers was 19 minutes (Schofields Station to Anson St, 1,149 m); where nothing is closer TfNSW walks further (67 minutes from Bobbin Head, 68 from Audley). Inferred from 25 against 30: a soft default somewhere around 25 to 29 minutes.
- What removing it loses (verified): nothing at 15 or 30. At 8, four small cases where it found a journey 0 to 6 minutes later with a final walk 4 to 5 minutes shorter, still over 8.
- School buses (verified): none in the 36 default answers; 13 of 36 answers at 15 and 5 of 20 at 8 used one, always 6627 days later. Default answers do use school buses when they run near the time asked for (Monday 14:30).
- Walking pace (verified): TfNSW walks about 1.1 m/s (Schofields Station to Anson St, 1,082 m in 16.2 minutes); HopReady's own walks assume 1.3 m/s over 1.3 times the straight line, so its estimate there is 13 minutes.
- On Demand (verified in responses): TfNSW returns On Demand D700 as product class 10 with icon 23; the adapter maps only classes 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 9 and 11, so those journeys are dropped (6 of 12 from Tallawong to Anson St at Monday 08:00). Not changed by ADR-0009.

## 2. What the literature, the engines and the other apps say (researched 2026-10-31)

Eight research notes were written on 2026-10-31 and every critical claim was re-checked by a second reader against the primary source. Only upheld claims appear here; where the checker narrowed a claim, the narrowed version is given and the downgrade is noted. Verified means the source was read or the computation was run; inferred means consistent with the sources but not stated by one; unknown means no source settles it. Sources are numbered in 2.7 and cited as [n].

### 2.1 How people choose a park-and-ride car park

Appraisal guidance gives weights, not cut-offs. TfNSW's Economic Parameter Values (January 2025) and the ATAP M1 technical report (August 2021) convert the uncomfortable parts of a trip into equivalent on-board minutes inside an additive generalised time; neither document sets a maximum acceptable walk or wait (verified [1][2]). ATAP on the old walking weight of 2.0: "This should no longer be used" (verified [2]). Wardman et al. (2026; 2,872 multipliers from 606 studies) support 1.75 for walking with waiting kept at 2.0 under revealed preference, or 1.5 for both under stated preference (verified [3]; the note's "1.75 for both" was corrected by the checker).

| Transfer waiting                 | 1.5 x                                                                 | [1]                 |

Revealed preference. Webb (2020; 1,690 park-and-ride users, 111 lots, Twin Cities) estimates in-car time at 4.2 times transit in-vehicle time (coefficients -0.39 and -0.092), a transfer at 1.61 (about 17.5 transit minutes), rail or BRT at +1.53 (about 16.6 transit minutes) and a directness term of -3.81 on the ratio of the origin-lot-destination distance to the direct distance, which acts mainly as a choice-set boundary (verified [4]). Walk time weighs less than transit time in that model (-0.067), so walking as a main disutility rests on the Austin study alone (verified [4][6]; checker correction). Users do not strictly minimise total time: the chosen lot averages 1.2 times the fastest available door-to-door time, and the drive times behind the 4x weight are free-flow, so the weight should not be imported literally for congested minutes (verified [4][5]; the checker narrowed "do not minimise" to "do not strictly minimise"). Pang and Khani (Austin) find agency designation and frequency of transit paths the strongest attractors, and transfers, auto time and walking the strongest disutilities (verified at abstract level [6]).

Bypassing the nearest station. In Perth only 30 to 80 percent of drive-to-rail commuters choose the nearest station; paid bays, access mode and time of day mattered more than distance, and two traveller classes exist, one seeking facilities and one seeking quick access (verified at abstract level [7][8]). TfNSW's 2015 qualitative research: most park at the interchange closest to home, but space availability and avoiding a connecting bus to reach a preferred express service change the choice (verified [9]). Before the metro opened, Cherrybrook and Castle Hill residents drove 2 to 4 km to Beecroft, Thornleigh, Seven Hills and Parramatta, so catchments spanned several lines (verified [17]). Catchments are parabolic or ellipse-like with the lot near the focus; willingness to backtrack is limited but not zero, and about half of users live within 4 km of the lot (verified [16]).

Full car parks. Transport Scotland (2012): for bus park-and-ride a 10 percent chance of no space cuts demand 19 percent and a 20 percent chance 34 percent; for rail a 0 to 20 percent chance cuts demand 4.3 percent; over 60 percent of bus users would drive the whole way if parking failed; park-and-ride users have relatively low values of time and frequency was insignificant where services were frequent (verified [14]). Perth stated-preference work finds users risk-averse to variation in parking search time and favouring stations with predictable parking (verified at abstract level [15]).

Sydney fill times. Every Sydney car park with a weekday fill time on record fills early, but the record covers only high-demand sites, is mostly pre-COVID, and comes from parties documenting a shortage; the 2018 inquiry found "a lack of detailed information on the occupancy rates of commuter car parks" (verified [10]; the note's "typically 7:00 to 8:00" was narrowed to the named sites, and the busiest fill by 7:00 to 7:30). TfNSW's own research found users leave early to secure a spot (verified [9]).

### 2.2 How routing engines handle car access

OpenTripPlanner 2. Park-and-ride is the street mode CAR_TO_PARK; it must be combined with transit and the car leg ends only at a parking area tagged in OSM (verified [21]). Every access limit is documented as a performance bound: accessEgress.maxDuration (default 45 min) "is a performance limit and should therefore be set high ... Use itinerary-filters to limit what is presented to the client"; the GraphQL plan arguments maxWalkDistance and maxPreTransitTime (the park-and-ride pre-transit cap) are deprecated with "Does nothing. Use walkReluctance instead" (verified [20][22]). Walking is a cost (walk.reluctance 2.0), never a cap. Car access is penalised by default since 2.4: a time penalty of 20 min plus 2.0 times the drive and a cost factor of 1.5 on car-to-park, car-pickup, car-rental and car-hailing legs, the real duration still shown to the user, plus car.reluctance 2.0, parking time 1 min and parking cost 120 (verified [20]). The motivation: "we would love to drive to the CLOSEST parking where we can board the trip we want, not drive all the way to the destination - just because it is 5 minutes faster" (verified [24]). The sandbox Vehicle-to-Stop heuristic, which truncated the nearest-first car search once reached stops scored 300 points (rail, ferry and subway 20 each, bus 1), was removed in OTP 2.5 (February 2024) because it "was returning very strange results"; the penalty and a flat stop cap (maxStopCount 500) replaced it (verified [23]; the note's reading of it as a connectivity-based candidate selection was refuted by the checker). OTP's own list of bad park-and-ride results (driving almost all the way then walking, driving to the edge of town then bussing back, very short drives) ends: "We currently have a bunch of filters but they don't interact well with each other and are pretty ad-hoc" (verified [25]). Raptor returns a Pareto set over arrival time, transfers, duration and generalised cost; a filter chain then groups itineraries sharing 85 percent of their legs, drops those costlier than "15m + 1.5 t" of the best, removes transit slower than walking, and raises an error only when nothing remains (verified [20][26]; the "500 paths" in the design document is an illustration, not a bound).

MOTIS v6 offers experimental CAR_PARKING and CAR_DROPOFF pre-transit modes with maxPreTransitTime defaulting to 900 s; its street router leaves the car only at nodes flagged as parking and charges a fixed switch penalty of 200 (verified [28][29]). Navitia takes car as a first-section mode ending in a park section, caps fallback at 30 min by default and tags each journey (best, rapid, comfort, car, less_fallback_walk, fastest), exactly one being best (verified [30]). Google's Routes API has no drive-to-station mode: transit routes use walking to and from stations, preferences are LESS_WALKING or FEWER_TRANSFERS ("should prefer"), and preferred modes may still be ignored "depending on the efficiency of the route" (verified [31][32]). The consumer app combines driving with transit as "Connecting modes" (Drive, Ride services, Bicycle) and groups results under "Public transport" and "Mixed-modes", "only available on mobile and in some locations" (verified [33][47]; Sydney coverage unknown). Rover's 2024 Dutch planner test found Google, NS and 9292 cannot cope well with car plus public transport: one park-and-ride advice only, car options below the fold, no parking location named, and NS optimising total time without parking time so sending people to Intercity stations with no parking; only Gaiyo named several car parks with prices and capacity (verified [34]). HSL's Reittiopas exposes park-and-ride as a car_park toggle with quick option sets least-transfers and least-walking (verified [35]).

Hard walking caps and result quality. The KIT study behind OTP's design (Delling, Dibbelt, Pajor, Wagner, Werneck 2012; 1,000 London queries) found that capping transfer walks at 10 min had "almost no effect on solution quality" (97 percent top-3), but also capping origin and destination walks at 15 min dropped quality to 38 percent (top-3) and 30 percent (top-6): "unacceptably low: the algorithm misses good journeys (including all-walk) quite often" (verified [27]). The checker's caveat: the metric is the paper's own fuzzy top-k match, the cap was 15 min at both ends on random short city pairs, so the direction transfers to HopReady's Parramatta case and the number does not. Transit (the app) is not evidence that walk limits are soft: its "Minimize walking" is a walk_reluctance cost of 2.1 against a default of 1.1, but its API applies a hard 1,500 m access radius by default and has a strict accessibility mode, and whether its 10 and 20 minute walk options drop trips is unknown (verified [37][38]; the note's "disabling a mode is Transit's only hard filter" was refuted).

### 2.3 How Apple Maps, Google Maps, Citymapper and Transit lay out results

Map plus sheet. Apple Maps on iOS 26 is one full-screen map with one resizable card: "Tap the search field (at the top of the card)", "drag the top of the card up or down"; Maps hides its floating buttons when the sheet expands, and iOS 26 half-height sheets are inset with a glass background that turns opaque at full height (verified [39][40][42]). The three-detent account (floating card, closer to the edges, full sheet) is Expo's hands-on description, not Apple's (inferred [44]). Google Maps moved directions into bottom sheets in 2024 to 2025: from and to float at the top, the mode carousel sits at the bottom "for improved reach", a strip of map stays visible at full height, and the iOS tab bar went from five tabs to three (verified [45]). The HIG asks for a grabber, a medium detent for progressive disclosure, one sheet at a time and an interactive map kept visible behind a place card (verified [41]); NN/g adds that non-modal sheets suit map apps because people keep referring to the background and that the grabber alone is a weak affordance (verified [54]).

Option row anatomy. Google's 2025 row: a large two-line duration on the left, "Arrive 8:52" with one reason phrase ("Fastest route, despite the usual traffic"), distance and tolls last, deliberately less dense (verified [46]). Apple's transit rows carry departure times, connections and fare where available, each ending in Go or Steps; tapping the row anywhere but Go opens the step list, and Apple never shows steps and alternatives at once (verified [39]). Citymapper shows about five rows ranked by speed, cost and transfers with a colour-coded chain of mode icons and the price; its "Best for you" view promotes two routes with a one-line reason and coloured tags and keeps a "Classic" list (verified [50][51]). Transit lists the best trip first as a one-line timebar; Transit 6.0 (August 2025) made the departure time 60 pt and folded alerts, crowding and cancellations into ETA cards; real-time departures carry two pulsing waves, scheduled ones are greyed, cancellations are crossed out (verified [52][53]).

Preferences as re-ranking behind one control. Apple exposes a single Prefer chip (bus, rail, ferry) below the destination and documents no walking limit, speed, fewer-transfers or less-walking setting (verified [39]). Google's transit preferences sit in a "Trip options" sheet reached from a chip row above the results (Now, Modes, Filter by, Connecting modes) or the Options button; Route options are single-choice (Best route, Fewer transfers, Less walking, Wheelchair-accessible, Lowest cost) and preferred modes are switches (verified [47][48]; the note's "one Options button" was corrected). Citymapper's Walk Less "ranks by duration of outdoor walk", Simple routes favour fewer transfers, speed or price re-rank the list, step-free is its one true filter, and hard changes are tagged "tight transfer" (verified [49][50]). TfNSW's own planner (February 2026) offers fastest, fewest changes and least walking as a refine choice with walking speeds of 2, 4 and 6 km/h; its help text loads client-side and was not read directly (inferred [55]). None of the four apps shows why a route is missing (inferred; no help page documents such an affordance). Of the planners surveyed, HopReady before ADR-0008 was the only one applying a walking limit as a rejection (verified against docs/design/routing.md).

Sort chips, time and tabs. Apple and Google have no per-route sort chips; the time control is one pill below the destination ("Now" opening Leave at or Arrive by; Transit's "Leave Now") (verified [39][48][52]). Citymapper puts the clock top-right and its routing powers as an icon row at the bottom (verified [49]). The HIG keeps tabs few, never hides them, asks that empty sections be explained, and reserves the tab bar accessory for persistent state such as a playing track or, here, a parked car (verified [43]). What follows for HopReady (inferred from the above): one from-to capsule, one time pill, four mode chips, at most five rows best-first with a reason tag and a "More options" row, the Sort by chip row replaced by a single-choice ranking preference behind one Options button, limits in Settings, the four-tab bar kept, and HopReady's own "car parks considered" list kept one tap away, since no competitor shows one and it is the direct answer to "why isn't Seven Hills here".

### 2.4 Provider facts that constrain the design

TfNSW Trip Planner API. The current swagger (version 10.6.21.17) and the April 2022 manual v3.3 document 29 parameters and no car, taxi, Kiss & Ride or Park & Ride request parameter (verified [56][57]). Mentz, the engine vendor, documents trITMOT values 100 (walk) to 105 (taxi), with 104 Park & Ride needing the macro prRoutingMacro=true (verified [58]). HopReady's -303 probe omitted that macro, so the documented absence is verified and the engine's capability is unknown; the design does not hinge on it, since EFA would choose the car park and time the drive without traffic (downgraded by the checker from "cannot plan park-and-ride"). trITMOTvalue100 is vendor-documented (default 10 min, gated by itOptionsActive=1) and only TfNSW omits it; "preference, not guarantee" is HopReady's inference from one probe (inferred [58]). Since 27 August 2025 every response reports version 10.6.21.17, the version parameter is ignored and stop_finder accepts only type_sf=any (verified [61]). Zero-journey responses carry systemMessages codes (-4006 JUST_WALK, -4000 NO_CONNECTION, -4303 walking settings, -2000 STOP_INVALID) and should be read through them (verified [56]). calcNumberOfTrips is a target, not a cap (verified [56][58]). No public source states latency (unknown).

Quotas. The Bronze plan is 60,000 requests a day and 5 a second per key, shared by every endpoint of the product, counter reset at midnight AEST, usage invisible to the developer (verified [59]). TfNSW documents 403 with X-Error-Detail for over-limit; developers have only ever reported 503 (2017 to 2022) and 429 (August 2025), so 401, 403, 429 and 503 are all possible over-limit signals (verified [60][62]; checker correction). Increases are free after a short description of the app; one was granted in about 17 hours in August 2025 (verified [60]). Whether the Car Park API shares the Trip Planner key's bucket is unknown.

Car Park API. The official swagger defines GET /carpark (facility list), /carpark?facility=<id>, /carpark/full-list and /carpark/history?facility&eventdate; the PDF (v2.4, September 2026) defines fields only (verified [63][64]). full-list, announced 30 April 2026, returns all 41 live facilities (34 locations) with tsn, spots, entry coordinate, occupancy.total and MessageDate; /carpark without a facility returns 46 entries, five of them historical metro records that full-list omits (verified [63][65]). Spot counts drift, 15 of 41 differ between the PDF and the 2026-10-28 feed, and the PDF says not to hard-code them (verified [63]). Zone data is "not accurate" for Ashfield, Kogarah, Seven Hills, Manly Vale and Brookvale, and Seven Hills' zones sum inconsistently, so only the facility total is usable (verified [63]). TfNSW's display rules: availability = spots minus total, "Full" below 1, "Almost Full" below 10 percent, prefix "~"; its own widget treats a feed older than 3,600 s as stale (verified [63][67]). Known hardware outages: Gordon frozen December 2024 to January 2025 (Skidata works), Bella Vista and Tallawong zones stuck full (2021, 2022), Brookvale wrong for days (2025), power outages at Schofields, Ashfield, Kogarah, Sutherland, Narrabeen and Mona Vale (2025 to 2026), Northern Beaches gates locked open 18 June to 2 July 2026 (verified [66]). Rules: up to 18 hours free when the same accepted card taps on and off within the parking period, else $30 weekdays or $10 weekends per 24 h; a contactless card qualifies only through Transport Connect; Dee Why closes overnight (verified [67]). The public ArcGIS commuter car park layer holds 2020 to 2021 data republished in 2024 and counts whole interchanges, not Park&Ride capacity, so it is no substitute for the feed (verified; the note's description of it as a 2024 snapshot and the only open capacity source was refuted).

Apple Maps Server API. /v1/etas takes one origin and up to 10 destinations per call (Automobile, Transit, Walking, Cycling; departureDate or arrivalDate, to be treated as mutually exclusive) and returns distanceMeters, expectedTravelTimeSeconds and staticTravelTimeSeconds (verified [69]). /v1/directions supports Automobile, Walking and Cycling only (verified [70]). The quota is 25,000 service calls a day per team shared with MapKit JS, plus 250,000 MapKit JS map views; 429 means the daily quota or, rarely, burst throttling; the only path to more is a sign-in-gated request form and no paid tier is published (verified [68][71][72]). Whether a 10-destination request counts as 1 call or 10 is unknown. Apple returns no transit legs, lines or timetables; a native MapKit probe did return a Transit ETA for a Sydney pair, but the server endpoint is untested for Sydney (verified for the probe, unknown for the server [68]). Unified Maps URLs (iOS 18.4 and later) open transit directions and the parked car at no quota cost (verified [73]).

GTFS. The complete TfNSW GTFS (312 MB zipped, 1.56 GB unzipped, two thirds shapes.txt, 91 days of service, CC BY 4.0) downloads without a key from the TfNSW CKAN resource URL and is mirrored daily by MobilityData as feed mdb-2449 (verified [74][75]; the checker found the TfNSW URL itself key-free, so MobilityData is a fallback, not a dependency). A 437-line Python RAPTOR built the whole-NSW weekday network in 9 s and answered a one-to-all query in 0.21 s, but the build peaks at 550 MB against the 128 MB Workers isolate limit, so a nightly matrix needs an external runner (verified [76]).

### 2.5 How good the hub-table estimate is

Method (run by the researcher, re-run by the checker): true times were computed by RAPTOR over the TfNSW GTFS for Thursday 5 November 2026 from 35 Park&Ride origins to 450 destinations (251 rail, metro and light rail stations plus 199 bus stops); the proxy was the minimum over 8 hubs (Central, Town Hall, Wynyard, Chatswood, Parramatta, Macquarie Park, North Sydney, Victoria Cross) of the table time plus the onward trip from that hub with a 2 min re-board (verified). The baseline is not exact (footpaths capped at 400 m; 1.7 percent of pairs where the proxy beat it), and a corrected rerun moved every headline figure by at most 0.3 min mean and 1.6 min p90 (verified).

Car park choice. Picking the best of a regional candidate set by the proxy finds the true best 85 percent of the time in a Hills set of 9 car parks (regret mean 3.1 min, p90 7.8, max 58) and 72 percent in a southern set (Sutherland, Revesby, Beverly Hills, Riverwood, Kogarah; regret p90 30, max 119) where no hub is near (verified; set membership differs slightly from the note's). With the proxy's top 5 checked exactly, as ADR-0007 does, the true best survives screening for 96 percent of destinations in the Hills set and 100 percent in the southern set, but only 54 percent across all 35 origins at once (verified). Regret is not always zero near a hub: an infrequent bus 2.2 km from a hub produced a 60 min regret (verified). The repo's table, recorded from the TfNSW Trip Planner, agrees with RAPTOR within 5 min for 84 percent of 238 origin-hub pairs, mean difference 1.6 min (verified [79]).

Why Seven Hills matters. From the GTFS and the T1 timetable valid from 30 October 2026, Seven Hills to Central is 33 min on board (express, 8 min between departures at 08:00) against 46 to 47 min from Kellyville by metro (4 min between departures in the peak [78]); to Chatswood and Macquarie Park the metro wins by 16 to 20 min; to North Sydney the two tie at 46 min in the recorded table (verified [77][79]; the note's "15 to 25 min" for North Sydney was corrected). These are station-to-hub minutes: Kellyville Station to Seven Hills Station is 9.3 km by road, about 11 min free-flow, so door to door the two are close and drive ETA plus occupancy decide (inferred). With the corrected on-board times and the note's drive, park and walk allowances, the five rail and metro options from Wrights Road at 08:00 span 52 to 65 min (Hills Showground 52, Cherrybrook 56 to 60, Seven Hills 57 to 61, Bella Vista 60, Kellyville 63 to 65), with North Rocks (139 spaces, bus to Wynyard then train) at 75 to 80 (inferred; the note's 40 min for Seven Hills and 223 spaces for North Rocks were refuted). In the recorded 28 October plans Seven Hills passed the direction test and was dropped by the four-candidate cap after a rough estimate; Tallawong was the car park marked not towards the destination (verified; the note's attribution to the direction rule was corrected).

Extra hubs. The researcher suggested Hurstville, Liverpool, Strathfield, Blacktown, Epping, Bondi Junction and Sydenham or Bankstown, untested. `apps/api/scripts/record-hub-times.ts` now lists Strathfield, Epping, Blacktown, Liverpool, Hurstville, Bondi Junction and Sydenham alongside the ten existing hubs; the table in `packages/core/src/data/park-and-ride-hub-times.ts` (recorded 2026-10-31) was re-recorded with them on 2026-10-31 in the evening: 34 stations by 17 hubs by 3 periods, 1,734 pairs, none missing (verified, recording log).

### 2.6 What this settles for HopReady

- Rank every documented car park by an estimated door-to-door time and check the best few exactly (docs/decisions/0007-rank-every-car-park-by-estimated-time.md). Engines with their own graph search every access stop within a generous bound and let cost decide (2.2); people weigh car minutes far above train minutes, prefer rail or metro to bus and bypass the nearest station for a surer space or a better service (2.1); the best car park flips with the destination (2.5). Direction is never a rule on its own.
- Choose which car parks to check with the generalised-cost weights and show plain minutes (2.1). HopReady applies walk 1.5 and wait 1.4 (`packages/core/src/estimate.ts`, 2026-10-31); the transfer penalties (7.2 to 14.8 IVT minutes) and the bus constant (about 6 to 10) need the mode and the changes on each hub route, which the hub table does not record yet, and the drive counts once because a heavier car weight favours the nearest car park (ADR-0007, alternatives).
- Preferences mark options, never hide them (docs/decisions/0008-preferences-mark-options-never-hide-them.md). No reference planner rejects on walk time, the one engine study that capped origin and destination walks measured quality falling to 38 percent, and TfNSW honours trITMOTvalue100 as a preference while still returning the 11 min walk when it is the only way to the station (2.2, 2.4, Part 1). The only hard filters with precedent are modes to avoid and step-free.
- Read occupancy for every facility from one full-list call, apply TfNSW's display rules, treat a feed older than an hour as stale, use facility totals not zones, and derive per-facility fill curves from the feed rather than a clock rule (2.1, 2.4). Availability is what separates options within 15 minutes of each other.
- Keep provider calls at about 2 Apple ETA and 5 to 8 TfNSW trips per plan inside 25,000 and 60,000 a day; treat 401, 403, 429 and 503 as possible over-limit signals; do not assume a 10-destination ETA counts as one call (2.4).
- Results screen: one map, one resizable sheet, one time pill, four mode chips, at most five best-first rows with a reason tag and marks for long walks, ranking preference behind one Options button, limits in Settings, "car parks considered" one tap away, no Sort by chips (2.3; docs/plans/routing-and-results-v2.md step 10).
- Revisit the hub table when plans to destinations far from a hub are common: a station-level GTFS matrix is cheap (under 1 MB, minutes a night) but needs a runner outside Workers (2.4, 2.5; ADR-0007 "Revisit if").
- Not settled: TfNSW's engine capability for park-and-ride routing (probe trITMOT=104 with prRoutingMacro=true and keep the raw systemMessages), Apple's Transit ETA for Sydney server-side, how Apple counts multi-destination ETA calls, and fill times for the roughly 30 facilities with none on record.
